# Space Defenders: 0.9 polish review

This iteration preserves the dual-station arcade game and authored artwork, while reducing startup friction and making its controls and combat feedback more predictable. Following playtest feedback, it restores the original space-opera presentation, shortens the opening crawl to ten seconds, keeps hand skeleton feedback visible, and removes the added pilot shield. It is a desktop playtest candidate; it still needs real-camera and cross-browser playtesting before being called production ready.

## Design direction

Preserve the authored Star Wars-inspired identity: gold typography, space backdrop, original logo and sprites, simple centered actions, and the short opening crawl. Keep information brief in the menu and available again during pause. Blue identifies Pilot and orange/red communicates heat or danger. Show warning text steadily and keep the cannon heat fill inside its fixed track. The visible skeleton overlay is deliberate gameplay feedback for camera alignment. Keep the gun-pose instruction consistent with that overlay. A selection outline and right-index pointer should make menu focus obvious; thumb-flick confirmation must not carry through into combat. Credits retain the themed crawl but return after fifteen seconds.

The menu adapts to small screens and zoom. Gameplay retains its 1280×720 dual-view canvas; touch controls and a fully responsive combat HUD are a separate iteration. The desktop requirement is stated before launch.

The [technology guide](technology.md) documents the resolved library versions, browser APIs, custom gesture/filter/menu implementation, feature-to-file mapping, test/build tooling and optional artwork pipeline.

## Loading and performance

- Original image files: 8,249,693 bytes; optimized files: 5,349,500 bytes. This saves transfer bytes, not decoded texture memory, because dimensions are intentionally unchanged.
- The approximately 7.46 MiB hand model and one approximately 10–11 MiB WASM variant load only after camera permission is granted.
- MediaPipe JavaScript is split into an on-demand chunk. Phaser remains the dominant initial bundle; the production build still reports its large-chunk advisory.
- The camera requests 640×480 frames at up to 30 FPS; inference ignores duplicate video frames and hidden tabs. The hand skeleton overlay is on by default and does not intercept pointer input.
- Inference still runs synchronously on the main thread. A worker-based pipeline needs profiling on lower-end hardware before implementation.
- Asset and model URLs use Vite's BASE_URL, enabling builds under a repository subpath.

## Next playtest priorities

1. **Physical camera testing:** verify left/right role mapping, thumb flick recognition, lighting tolerance, GPU and CPU delegates, permission recovery, and reconnect behavior. Automated lifecycle tests cannot establish gesture quality.
2. **Balance and difficulty:** play complete runs at 30/60/120 Hz. Confirm the six-second cannon, cooling lockout and new damage budgets feel fair against meteors, beams, missiles and boss attacks. Pilot should survive five isolated full-health meteor hits; the sixth can defeat it. Gunner impacts should not consume multiple lives inside the 900 ms recovery window. Introduce a gentle first-wave ramp if onboarding remains difficult.
3. **Collision/art audit:** most sprites contain substantial transparent margins. Draw temporary hitbox overlays and align collision shapes with artwork; normalize animation pivots before cropping or resizing. This pass only adjusts the known beam mismatch.
4. **HUD consolidation:** preserve both stations' identity while making hull, gunner defenses/lives, heat and targeting visually consistent. Confirm readability at 1366×768 and 200% zoom.
5. **Audio and accessibility:** add intentional firing/lock/impact cues, then a working mute control; provide reduced shake settings and evaluate color-independent threat cues. Add touch play only with a coherent control layout.
6. **Long-session profiling:** measure FPS, inference latency, object counts and memory during real twenty-minute runs on integrated graphics; use atlases/object pools or worker inference where measurements justify them.

### Gesture, balance and responsiveness follow-up

The current suite has 37 passing tests. The latest Chromium runs verified a fresh-visit gesture choice, real no-camera fallback without any model requests, cancellation of pending camera permission and release of a late stream, and preserved gesture mode across home/credits/mission transitions. Simulated right-index/thumb-flick input selected native and Phaser menus, recovered separate Pilot and Gunner hand-loss pauses, aligned the selector with a letterboxed 1440×900 canvas, and resumed without firing a gameplay shot. Retry reset hull/defenses/lives and removed the menu overlay. Holding a mouse retry click also produced no shot; a fresh click still fired normally.

A real scene collision burst of twelve meteors/missiles left Pilot hull at 82%, Gunner defenses at 75%, and all three lives intact. Normal credits returned after about 15.02 seconds; reduced-motion credits returned after about 15.09 seconds. An early credits exit cancelled its timer without interrupting later Settings navigation. The opening crawl still lasted about ten seconds. A delayed asset request could be exited with gestures during preload; a deliberately failed asset could be retried with gestures. Loading-step listeners and recovery overlays were removed after exit/retry. No uncaught browser errors occurred; the intentional HTTP 404 in the asset recovery test was expected.

The redundant detection timer could lower nominal 30 FPS tracking toward 20 FPS because of display timing; detection now follows unique camera frames. Synthetic cadence, stationary-jitter and full input-response tests guard the fix. Browser scene updates with supplied hand coordinates reached 90% aim response within 67 ms and active Pilot response within 100 ms across 30/60/120/144 Hz, excluding the hand filter and camera/inference stages. The full smoothing-path test includes the hand filter and enforces a 250 ms budget. Actual camera recognition, end-to-end latency, cross-browser behavior and long-session performance still require representative hardware playtesting.


### Instruction follow-up

The pause/disconnected-hand menu now uses its left side for Pilot grab and open-palm release diagrams, and its right side for Gunner pointer/fire and fist laser cannon diagrams. These code-native SVG poses follow the owner's hand-skeleton references and use the existing overlay's blue joints. They scale with the gameplay canvas and leave the central resume/mode/menu actions available. A four-second gold Star Wars-style mission briefing shows the same gestures before both fresh launches and retries, ending with “Good luck, Pilot. May the Force be with you.” The combat scenes and mission clock start after the briefing; leaving it cancels the launch timer.

Chromium measured fresh launch at approximately 4.05 seconds and retry at 4.07 seconds, with both combat scenes inactive during the briefing. The four pose diagrams remained on their correct sides and did not overlap menu actions at 1280×720 or on a letterboxed 1440×900 canvas. Simulated hand recovery resumed with the guides removed; a held retry click produced no shot. Esc cancellation stayed in Settings past the original launch deadline, and gesture Back removed the briefing without leaking guide or cursor overlays. The 37 existing tests, TypeScript check and production build pass.
